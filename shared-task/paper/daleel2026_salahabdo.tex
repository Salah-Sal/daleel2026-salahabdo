\begin{abstract}
We describe the SalahAbdo closed-track systems for both Daleel 2026
subtasks: deciding which argumentative-unit types appear in an Arabic
paragraph (Task~1) and locating their exact spans (Task~2), in
editorials and debates. No single method was best for all six
argumentative-unit types, so our Task~1 system routes each type to the
method that measured best for it: assumption (\as), testimony (\te),
and statistics (\st) are detected by sampling a \texttt{gemma-4-31b}
classifier five times and firing when enough samples agree; anecdote
(\an) and other (\ot) by a fine-tuned CAMeLBERT classifier; and the
lowest-agreement type, common ground (\co), by ranking paragraphs and
firing a fixed count. A final verifier shows the LLM examples of true and false
\st{} paragraphs and removes likely false positives. Task~2 first
extracts spans as verbatim LLM quotes, then re-labels them by blending
encoder probabilities with label-sequence statistics under Viterbi
decoding, with zero additional LLM calls. Every design change had to
clear an adoption bar fixed before any experiment ran: 29 recorded
decisions produced six adoptions, and composition arithmetic
predicted two development scores digit-for-digit. Development scores
are 0.7089 macro-$F_1$ (Task~1) and 0.7247 span $F_1$ (Task~2); the
pinned recipes scored 0.6499 and 0.7055 on test, and our final official
submissions scored \textbf{0.712} and \textbf{0.7316}: \textbf{first of
ten} and \textbf{fifth of eight} closed-track teams
(\S\ref{sec:results} discloses the evaluation-phase protocol).
Residual errors track the scheme's measured
human agreement ($\kappa$ as low as $0.114$): types whose definitions a
model can check against the text improved under LLM verification, while
types defined by annotation convention resisted every prompting attack
and were learned only from supervision and sequence structure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Argument mining is ``the automatic identification and extraction of the
structure of inference and reasoning expressed as arguments presented in
natural language'' \citep{lawrence-reed-2019-argument}. Daleel 2026
\citep{nabhani-etal-2026-daleel} asks for its first two steps
\citep{peldszus-stede-2013-argument} in Arabic: find the argumentative
discourse units (ADUs) in a paragraph and decide which of six types
each one is, in two genres. Prior Arabic resources target stance
detection
\citep{alhindi-etal-2021-arastance,alturayeif-etal-2022-mawqif} and
debate component classification
\citep{khader-etal-2024-munazarat,alzawqari-etal-2025-neural}, not this
six-type evidence-unit scheme. The task is hard in a measurable way:
whether a clause is \emph{common ground} or an \emph{assumption} is
implicit content that, as \citet{moens-2018-argumentation} observes
(quoted in the survey), ``resides in the mind of communicator and
audience.'' Even trained annotators agreed only slightly on such labels
($\kappa{=}0.114$; \S\ref{sec:task}).

\paragraph{Criteria can be checked; conventions cannot.} Some Daleel
labels are defined by criteria a model can \emph{check} against the text:
a statistics (\st) unit contains numbers or study results, a testimony
(\te) unit attributes a statement to a source. Others are defined by
conventions with no text-checkable rule to appeal to: whether shared
background is common ground (\co) rather than an assumption (\as), or a
settled event an anecdote (\an) rather than \co, is a line the
annotation team drew. The distinction predicted our results: every
LLM verification stage aimed at a checkable label worked, every
prompting attack on a convention label failed, and convention labels
entered the system only through a fine-tuned encoder and sequence
structure.

\paragraph{Every change had to clear a bar set in advance.} A change
was adopted only if it beat a score bar fixed before its results
existed, with the design and both planned outcomes recorded in a
ledger; failed changes were closed for good (\S\ref{sec:method}; all
twenty-nine in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). This caught two changes that
looked good internally but failed externally, and predicted two
development scores digit-for-digit (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~A Task~1 system that routes each label to
the method that measured best for it, plus a verifier for the one label
whose problem was precision (dev macro-$F_1$ 0.6159 $\rightarrow$
0.7089). (2)~A Task~2 re-labeling stage that improves an LLM span
pipeline by $+0.0400$ dev $F_1$ with no additional LLM calls, plus a
placebo ablation separating structure's contribution from the
encoder's. (3)~A gated adoption methodology for shared-task
conditions, publishing the gate registry, negative results, transfer
ledger, and an analysis tying residual errors to measured human
agreement and vote statistics. Code, prompts, scorer ports, and
analysis artifacts:
\url{https://github.com/Salah-Sal/daleel-2026-arabic-argumentative-discourse-mining-shared-task}
(public at camera-ready).

\section{Task, Data, and the Human Ceiling}
\label{sec:task}

\paragraph{Setup.} Daleel 2026 \citep{nabhani-etal-2026-daleel} provides
Arabic paragraphs from two genres: news editorials and transcribed
competitive debates (the Munazarat lineage;
\citealp{khader-etal-2024-munazarat,alsharafi-etal-2025-arabic}). Task~1
asks for the \emph{set} of ADU types present in a paragraph (six labels,
multi-label, empty sets legal); Task~2 asks for exact typed character
spans. Example (editorial, translated): ``Apple says privacy lies at the
core of all its products, yet these products come at elevated prices''
$\rightarrow$ Task~1 $\{\te,\co\}$; Task~2 a \te{} span over the Apple
claim and a \co{} span over the pricing clause. Task~1 is scored by
macro-$F_1$ over all six labels; Task~2 by a partial-match span $F_1$ in
which each overlapping same-label (prediction, gold) pair adds
overlap/len(prediction) to precision and overlap/len(gold) to recall
(scorer ports parity-tested; Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The training
release contains 612 paragraphs (357 debate, 255 editorial; 2{,}975 gold
spans covering 91\% of all text); the development input contains 217 and
the test input 213. Label priors are heavily skewed: \as{} appears in
75\% of paragraphs, other (\ot) 36\%, \an{} 30\%, \te{} 26\%, \co{} 6\%, \st{}
5\%. For all 612 paragraphs the Task~1 label set equals the Task~2
span-label set, so one span model serves both tasks. We split the 612
training paragraphs into a 430-paragraph working pool (five
genre-balanced folds; Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) and a 182-paragraph
held-out check. \emph{Out-of-fold} (OOF) scoring fits on four folds and
predicts the fifth; all design decisions used OOF scores on the 430,
the 182 serving only as a sanity guard (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{The scheme and its measured ceiling.} The six types, common
ground (\co), assumption (\as), testimony (\te), statistics (\st),
anecdote (\an), other (\ot), are those of the Webis-Editorials-16
annotation model \citep{alkhatib-etal-2016-news}, whose original
annotation study \emph{measured} per-type agreement (Fleiss' $\kappa$ at
segment level, before annotator judgments were merged into gold labels):
from 0.613 for \as{} down to \textbf{0.114} for \co{} --- slight
agreement \citep{landis-koch-1977-measurement,fleiss-1971-measuring};
full table in \S\ref{sec:criteria}. Given one annotator chose \co, the probability
another chose \as{} is 0.562, and \co/\ot{} are also the rarest types in
the source corpus (1.7\%/1.2\% of 14{,}313 units): low agreement and
extreme rarity together bound what any system can learn. We extrapolate
a pooled Task~2 ceiling of ${\approx}0.75$ (our estimate, not a
published number) and read residual errors against it.

\section{Task 1: Routed Multi-Source Composition}
\label{sec:t1}

No single method was best for all six labels
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}), so our final Task~1 system
(\textbf{v2.2}) \emph{routes} each label to the source that
measured best for it, with every threshold fitted on training folds and
applied to held-out folds (Table~\ref{tab:router}).

\paragraph{Sources.} \emph{(a) Self-consistency votes:} the frozen
zero-shot chain-of-thought classifier (\texttt{gemma-4-31b-it}, winner
of a pre-registered 14-model bake-off) is sampled $k{=}5$ times at
$T{=}0.7$ \citep{wang-etal-2023-selfconsistency}; a label fires when at
least $\theta_L$ of the five samples propose it ($k{=}5$ suffices:
\S\ref{sec:votes}). \emph{(b) Encoder:} CAMeLBERT-MSA-quarter
\citep{inoue-etal-2021-camelbert} fine-tuned to output a probability per
label per paragraph (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), with decision thresholds
fitted on OOF probabilities; the quarter-size variant was chosen in
advance on external evidence \citep{alzawqari-etal-2025-neural}.
\emph{(c) Span-derived labels:} the Task~2 pipeline's label sets, used
only in the \an{} union rule and the \co{} ranker.

\paragraph{The \st{} verifier.} An OOF error audit showed \st{} recall
already at 0.947 (18 of 19 golds found) while 19 of 37 fired paragraphs
were false positives: a pure precision problem. The verifier shows the LLM what true and false \st{}
paragraphs look like: for a fired paragraph, its prompt holds the
official definition plus all gold-\st{} paragraphs and all fired false
positives from the \emph{other} folds ($\sim$15 each) as labeled
examples; three samples at $T{=}0.7$ vote. A paragraph is dropped only
if the verifier majority says absent \emph{and} an independent model
family (qwen3-32b or llama-3.3-70b) also omits \st{} --- a two-key rule.
On OOF it dropped 10 paragraphs, all false positives (\st{} $F_1$ 0.6429
$\rightarrow$ 0.7826); on dev it dropped 2, again both false positives
(0.7619 $\rightarrow$ 0.8421). The question is checkable, so
verification transfers.

\paragraph{The \co{} budget.} Nothing we tried could actually detect
\co{} (\S\ref{sec:criteria}), and for a label this uncertain,
over-predicting is exactly what macro-$F_1$ rewards
\citep{lipton-etal-2014-thresholding}. So we control how
\emph{much} fires rather than \emph{what}: paragraphs are ranked by the
mean rank of three signals (encoder probability, vote fraction, span
presence), and the top $2\times$ training-frequency count is fired --- a
count, unlike a threshold, stays stable when scores drift across
datasets (cf.\ \citealp{lin-lin-2023-thresholding}).

\section{Task 2: Span Pipeline + Structural Re-Decode}
\label{sec:t2}

\paragraph{v3: proposal $\rightarrow$ atom $\rightarrow$ role.} LLMs are
unreliable at character arithmetic, so the model never outputs offsets:
the extraction stage asks for (label, \emph{verbatim quote}) pairs, and
a staged aligner locates each quote in the paragraph (exact,
whitespace-tolerant, orthography-normalized, then fuzzy; re-aligning
gold spans' own text scores $F_1$ 0.999). The extracted text
is then split at clause boundaries and connectives, and a classifier
(\textsc{SpanRoleDecision}) labels each piece with one of the six types
or \lab{none}; its prompt is the campaign's only adopted
prompt-optimizer product (GEPA \citep{agrawal-etal-2026-gepa} under
DSPy \citep{khattab-etal-2024-dspy}; prompt selection never saw the
scored fold). Extraction misses little: at most $4.2\%$ of any label's
gold mass falls outside every predicted span, so residual Task~2 error
is overwhelmingly mislabeling of correctly extracted text.

\paragraph{S1: the structural re-decode.} v3 labels each extracted span
\emph{independently}, yet gold labels are strongly sequential: an \an{}
span follows another \an{} 39\% of the time, against an 11.5\% base
rate. S1 therefore re-labels each paragraph's span sequence in five
frozen steps: (1)~a fine-tuned CAMeLBERT segment classifier gives each
span a probability distribution over the six labels; (2)~each
label's probability is rescaled by the
ratio (gold share of text)\,/\,(expected share under the encoder) ---
the $\tau{=}1$ point of logit adjustment \citep{menon-etal-2021-logit};
(3)~each span's evidence is $p = (1{-}\lambda)\,p_{\text{enc}} +
\lambda\,\text{onehot}(\text{v3 label})$, $\lambda{=}0.9$: the v3 label
keeps most of the weight;
(4)~Viterbi decoding \citep{viterbi-1967-error} picks the most probable
label sequence under genre-specific transition matrices estimated on
training folds (Laplace $\alpha{=}1$); (5)~Rule-A: a
decoded \co{} span must also appear in an independent zero-shot run,
else it takes that run's dominant label. Everything is fitted per fold,
and the bundle
changes only labels, never boundaries, so segmentation credit is
preserved by construction \citep{widmoser-etal-2021-randomized}.

\paragraph{What S1 fixed.} OOF pooled $F_1$ moved 0.6934 $\rightarrow$
0.7206 ($+0.0272$, better on all 5 folds), changing 237 of 3{,}824 span
labels. A \emph{placebo ablation} --- the identical decode with the
encoder replaced by uniform distributions --- scores 0.7133: structure
alone contributes $\sim$$+0.02$, encoder evidence the remaining
$+0.0073$ (cf.\ the $\neg$DAPT control of
\citealp{gururangan-etal-2020-dont}). What the decode repairs is
over-prediction of rare labels: predicted \co{} text fell from
$3.98\times$ the gold amount to $1.78\times$ and \st{} from $1.38\times$
to $1.07\times$. The declared limit: predicted \an{} text sits at
$0.48\times$ gold before and $0.49\times$ after: most gold \an{} text lies
inside larger spans of another label, which re-labeling cannot split.

\section{Adoption Discipline}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Register, then run.} The adoption bar entered version
control before any experiment ran; each candidate change was then
written into the ledger: design, grid, baseline, and both planned
outcomes. Where the entry's commit precedes its run --- both Task~2
adoptions among them --- git order itself proves the plan came first;
elsewhere design and verdict entered git together and precedence rests
on the ledger (marked in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}). This is
\emph{preregistration}: committing to the plan before the results exist
\citep{vanmiltenburg-etal-2021-preregistering}. (Ten early decisions
predate the ledger.)

\paragraph{The gate.} A change is adopted only if the composed system's
OOF score improves by at least $+0.02$ on the official metric \emph{and}
wins on at least 3 of the 5 folds. The margin tracks measured noise
(identical LLM configurations rerun at $\pm0.03$--$0.04$; estimated
composed-OOF band $\sigma{\approx}0.015$); smaller gains are
indistinguishable from it. The fold rule blocks adopting a spike on one
lucky fold (\co{} has 25 positives, \st{} 19). Every gate run
recomputes its baseline from identical inputs and aborts on mismatch;
a rejected family closes permanently: 29 decisions, six adoptions,
zero reopened families.

\paragraph{One bundled probe; external checks are sacred.} Each adopted
system earned exactly one development-set submission; nothing was tuned
against the leaderboard. Two failures justify it: an encoder swap that
won a 4-way internal comparison collapsed on dev
($0.6955{\rightarrow}0.6364$), and a recomposed \ot{} source reversed
($-0.10$ on its label); both were reverted. The 182-paragraph check
proved unreliable too (its champion readout missed dev by $-0.087$), so
composed OOF on the 430 became the honest predictor.

\paragraph{Exact composition arithmetic.} Macro-$F_1$ averages six
per-label scores and the router decides each label independently, so
swapping one label's source moves the system score by an exactly
computable amount. This let us \emph{predict} development scores: v2.1
and v2.2 were predicted 0.6955 and 0.7089, and scored exactly that
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Tables~\ref{tab:transfer} and~\ref{tab:progression} give the transfer
ledger and progression;
adopted changes transferred, S1's dev gain ($+0.0400$)
\emph{exceeding} its OOF gain ($+0.0272$). On test (213 paragraphs) the
pinned recipes scored 0.6499 and 0.7055, the Task~2 move from dev
($-0.019$) inside the noise band. The Task~1 drop ($-0.059$) decomposes
by label, not by system: \as, \st{} and \ot{} improved on test, while
the loss concentrates where frozen operating points met a shifted test
set (\co, \te, \an); Appendix~\ref{app:analysis} decomposes both.

\paragraph{Evaluation-phase protocol (post-freeze, disclosed).} When the
organizers released the development gold labels and extended the
deadline, we waived our own freeze for that window and ran one
time-boxed, leaderboard-informed campaign: four probes per task in one
evening plus two closing Task~2 uploads.
Per-label scorer feedback moved the \as, \te, \an, and \co{} sourcing
decisions across probes (Task~1 0.6499 $\rightarrow$ 0.712; Task~2
0.7055 $\rightarrow$ 0.7316, via span-drop filters keyed to Task~1
labels and encoder probabilities). Two stronger configurations missed
the board through operational error (Appendix~\ref{app:disclosure}). In
the organizers' final standings we place \textbf{first of ten}
closed-track teams on Task~1 and \textbf{fifth of eight} on Task~2
(leader 0.755). Our Task~1 lead is $3{\times}10^{-5}$, far inside our
own noise band: we read the top two as tied. We report these gains
separately: our
methodological claims rest on the gated development phase, not the
probe campaign.

\paragraph{Analysis.}\label{sec:analysis} Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}
develops it: prompting fails on convention labels while verification
transfers on checkable ones (\S\ref{sec:criteria}); $k{=}5$ votes
suffice and rare-label operating points sit on needles
(\S\ref{sec:votes}); S1's residual mirrors the human confusion matrix
(\S\ref{sec:families}). Paid API spend was ${\approx}$US\$60
\citep{dodge-etal-2019-show}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Both systems place every component where measured evidence routed it,
and every adoption cleared a pre-declared gate; the ranks matter less
than the ledger behind them. At this scheme's annotation ceiling \emph{verification}
helps where definitions are checkable, while convention labels move
only under supervision, priors, and structure. The registry guards
against the winner's curse; extraction is the unreached lever.

\section*{Limitations}

All selection statistics rest on a 430-paragraph OOF pool with 25 \co{}
and 19 \st{} positives; our estimated composed-OOF noise band is
$\sigma{\approx}0.015$, and our $+0.02$ gate is calibrated to it, not to
a universal standard. The ${\approx}0.75$ Task~2 ceiling is our
extrapolation from the source corpus's agreement study (a different
language, granularity, and consolidation stage) and serves as an
interpretive anchor, not a bound; the comparison with the human
confusion matrix in \S\ref{sec:families} sets differently normalized
quantities side by side, so it is directional, not exact. LLM legs depend on a specific serving stack: the same
checkpoint scored $0.048$ lower on a different provider and identical
configurations rerun at $\pm0.03$--$0.04$; pinning the provider, caching
artifacts, and count-based rare-label rules reduce but do not eliminate
this risk. The \st{} verifier's exemplar block is label-sorted, which
long-context evidence suggests is suboptimal
\citep{bertsch-etal-2024-manyshot}; the recipe was dev-validated as-is.
The TAPT (task-adaptive pretraining) measurement is single-seed. Our systems are tuned to
this scheme's conventions by design; we make no
claim that the specific routing transfers to other argument-annotation
schemes (``each data set appears to conceptualize claims quite
differently,''
\citealp{daxenberger-etal-2017-essence,lawrence-reed-2019-argument}).
Finally, the evaluation-phase probe campaign was leaderboard-informed
(\S\ref{sec:results}); we report it separately and do not claim its
gains as evidence for the methodology.

\section{Closed-Track Disclosure}
\label{app:disclosure}

Per the task rules, all models, tools, prompts, and processing steps:

\paragraph{Models (all open-weight, ${\leq}70$B, licenses verified).}
\texttt{gemma-4-31b-it} \citep{gemmateam-2026-gemma4}: every LLM stage
(Task~1 classifier, quote proposals, role decisions, \st{} verifier);
Apache-2.0; served via OpenRouter (paid route pinned after free-route
starvation); $T{=}0$ for reference/deploy runs, $T{=}0.7$ for rollouts;
ChatAdapter with a deterministic retry ladder (a JSON-adapter failure
mode on ${\sim}2.8\%$ of atom decisions was eliminated this way; a rare
whitespace-flood degeneration on ${\sim}1/430$ paragraphs is contained as
an empty prediction). Pretraining cutoff precedes the task's label
release, so gold-label leakage is impossible; the underlying news/debate
text may predate the cutoff. \texttt{qwen3-32b}
\citep{qwenteam-2025-qwen3} and \texttt{llama-3.3-70b}
\citep{llamateam-2024-llama3}: one zero-shot Task~1 run each, used only
as the verifier's co-signal (and in rejected bundle experiments);
$T{=}0$, which deviates from Qwen's recommended stochastic sampling;
Arabic is not among Llama~3's officially supported languages: we use it
only as a redundant negative signal; Llama~3.3 has no dedicated report,
so we cite the herd paper, and the exact checkpoint is identified by its
pinned route in the released configs. CAMeLBERT-MSA-quarter
\citep{inoue-etal-2021-camelbert}, Apache-2.0, pinned to an immutable
revision: Task~1 paragraph classifier and Task~2 segment classifier.
DSPy 3.3.0b1 \citep{khattab-etal-2024-dspy}; GEPA compile used
\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} as the reflection model (compile-time only; no task
inference). No external labeled data; no dataset text in any committed
artifact.

\paragraph{Prompts.} All prompts are audit-informed zero-shot seeds with
the official label definitions in a single canonical phrasing (shared
between programs and metric feedback); the \st{} verifier and rejected
\co{} specialist add fold-external labeled exemplars as described in the
text. Verbatim prompt texts ship in the released repository (made public
at camera-ready; see \S\ref{sec:intro}).

\paragraph{Evaluation-phase upload record (\S\ref{sec:results}).}
Stronger configurations missed the leaderboard on both tasks. Our final
Task~1 package (predicted 0.7182 by the composition arithmetic) was
never uploaded: the closing upload meant to carry it held an earlier
probe configuration by mistake (scoring 0.711) and was never pushed to
the board, so the scored Task~1 entry is probe-3 (0.712). Two closing
Task~2 uploads scored 0.733 but were never pushed either, leaving
probe-4 (0.7316) the scored Task~2 entry. Both are operational errors,
not modeling choices, and neither is claimed as a result.

\paragraph{Pre/post-processing.} Whitespace-only gold spans dropped
(training side only); Task~1 gold re-derived from span labels with parity
assertion; deterministic clause/connective atomization for role
decisions; verbatim-quote alignment (exact/whitespace/normalized/fuzzy
ladder) for offsets; per-label vote thresholds, encoder thresholds, \co{}
budget, calibration weights, blend $\lambda$, and transition matrices all
cross-fitted per fold on the 430-pool OOF artifacts and refit on all 430
for deployment twins; exact-duplicate span dedup after decode. Empty
prediction sets are legal outputs everywhere.

\section{Analysis Detail}
\label{app:analysis}

The four subsections below are the full development of the patterns
summarized in \S\ref{sec:analysis}.

\subsection{Criteria verify; conventions require supervision or structure}
\label{sec:criteria}

The campaign ran four prompting attacks on convention-defined labels, all
pre-declared, all failed: (i)~zero-shot \co{} definitions
(cross-fitted $F_1$ 0.07); (ii)~a \co{} definition judge as a fourth rank
feature (0.3733 vs.\ 0.40 control); (iii)~a \co{} contrastive many-shot
specialist with the annotators' own exemplars and four induced
not-\co{} conventions in context (0.3733
again: the model argued false positives \emph{into} \co{} while quoting
the rules); (iv)~a span-level \an-vs-\as{} judge (flip precision 0.44
where ${\geq}0.75$ was needed). Meanwhile the
\st{} verifier (aimed at a checkable
criterion) validated twice and shipped. The Webis agreement numbers
explain the asymmetry (Fleiss' $\kappa$, segment-level,
pre-consolidation: \as{} 0.613, \te{} 0.591, \st{} 0.582, \an{} 0.399,
\ot{} 0.152, \co{} 0.114; \citealp{alkhatib-etal-2016-news}): \co's
convention ($\kappa{=}0.114$, 0.562 confusion into \as{} \emph{among
humans}) is statistical, not definitional; there is nothing for a
verifier to check. Consistently,
gemma fired \co{} on \emph{every} call for 41 paragraphs
whose gold is not \co, and on \emph{no} call for 7 gold \co{} paragraphs:
a \emph{biased coin} no amount of
voting, prompting, or calibration can flip. A dev example makes the
convention concrete: the router fired \co{} on a debate opening that
merely \emph{frames} the motion (``this House would ban for-profit
companies from using social and political causes in marketing''): gold
\as{}/\ot, because a motion is assumed for the sake of debate, not
shared ground. Over-prediction of such
labels is even metric-rational \citep{lipton-etal-2014-thresholding}.

\subsection{Vote-scaling: why $k{=}5$, and why voting cannot fix \st{} or \an}
\label{sec:votes}

With $q_p$ the observed fraction of the five rollouts firing on paragraph
$p$, Table~\ref{tab:votes} turns three design choices into consequences
of vote statistics
\citep{chen-etal-2024-llmcalls,wang-etal-2023-selfconsistency,loo-2025-selfconsistency-plateau}.
(1)~\emph{$k{=}5$ suffices:} for the three vote-routed labels, $F_1$ at
$k{=}5$ with an optimal threshold is over $99\%$ of the infinite-vote
limit (${\geq}96.9\%$ for all six); more same-prompt rollouts buy almost
nothing. (2)~\emph{Voting cannot fix
\st:} the sampler already finds every gold \st{} ($p_1{=}0.99$, zero
inverted golds), but 26 non-gold paragraphs fire on a majority of
calls: correlated false positives that more votes make \emph{more}
confident. Precision required an orthogonal signal: the verifier.
(3)~\emph{Voting cannot fix \an:} half the gold \an{} paragraphs fire on
a minority of calls (inv$^{+}{=}0.50$), capping the infinite-vote
ceiling at 0.611, \emph{below} the supervised encoder's 0.717; routing
\an{} to the encoder is not a preference but an inequality. Finally,
deployed thresholds sit at their pooled-OOF optima
(Table~\ref{tab:lipton}), but the interval retaining ${\geq}95\%$
of peak $F_1$ spans 0.77 of the score range for \as{} against 0.016 for
\st{} and \textbf{0.006} for \co: rare-label operating points sit on
needles, and any dev$\rightarrow$eval score shift falls off the
peak: the quantitative case for \co's count budget and \st's verifier.

\subsection{What S1 did, seen against the human matrix}
\label{sec:families}

Grouping the six types into coarse families (evidence \te/\st/\an,
subjective \co/\as, other) localizes S1's contribution: the within-family
share of misassigned char mass fell 0.258 $\rightarrow$ 0.182 while
family-level $F_1$ barely moved (0.7512 $\rightarrow$ 0.7541): S1's
gain is within-family disambiguation. The residual concentrates in the
one cross-family confusion the decoder cannot reach, gold-\an{} mass
labeled \as{} (0.330): on dev, the gold \an{} span ``the reaffirmation
to activate the Loss and Damage Fund'' (a settled
event, hence anecdote by convention) was absorbed into a larger \as{}
span. The same confusion leads the human matrix (0.277;
\citealp{alkhatib-etal-2016-news}), and our 6-label OOF (0.7206) against
the 3-family score (0.7541) bounds what finer typing can still add
(\S\ref{sec:task}).

\paragraph{Per-label test decomposition (\S\ref{sec:results}).} Task~1
test $F_1$: \as{} 0.9408, \st{} 0.9286 (recall 1.0, so the verifier's
zero drops were correct), \ot{} 0.7805, \te{} 0.6724 (editorial recall
0.258 vs.\ 0.795 debate), \an{} 0.4628 (76 fired against 45 golds), \co{}
0.1143 (the frozen budget fired 25 paragraphs against 10 test golds, the
recorded overfiring risk realized). Editorial \ot{} scored 0.0 on 6
supports, the blind spot dev had already exposed.

\subsection{Negative results}
\label{sec:negative}

Every rejected family and its gate is in Appendix~\ref{app:gates}; the
sharpest three: the \textbf{cross-model vote bundle} delivered every
banked component's estimate almost exactly and the honest sum was still
$+0.0160 < +0.02$: closed without a probe, exactly the case the
winner's-curse guard exists for; a \textbf{shuffled-graph placebo}
scored within 0.0003 of the true discourse forest: the
structured-prompt gain was graph-shape, not attachment; and
\textbf{transductive TAPT} \citep{gururangan-etal-2020-dont} lifted the
raw encoder $+0.0476$ OOF but the composed system only
$+0.0066$: textbook composition dilution (the encoder feeds only
\an/\ot{} and \co{} rank features). Also closed at their gates: prompt
optimizers on monolithic signatures ($-0.04$ to $+0.003$; the only win
came after decomposition to single decisions), a decision-level judge
compile ($+0.033$ selection, $+0.001$ confirmation), learned stacking,
learned span arbitration, calibration temperature, and synthetic-label
routes
\citep{xu-etal-2023-supericl,fu-etal-2021-spanner,zhang-etal-2024-linkner,yung-etal-2025-synthetic,zhang-etal-2025-boxwrench,dipalo-etal-2024-pgkd,xiao-etal-2023-freeal}.

\section{Reproducibility and Cost}
\label{app:repro}

Every run writes a hash-bound artifact set (exact config, data hashes,
fold manifest, metrics, provenance, completion marker); adoption reports
verify fold membership by hash; deployment twins reuse the immutable
model/threshold contracts of their OOF runs. Split seeds: 20260709 for
the stratified 430/182 selection/confirmation split, 20260710 for the
5-fold paragraph-grouped, genre-stratified partition of the 430. Encoders are pinned to
40-hex revisions and trained fp32 on CPU for bit-stable deployment
parity (Task~1: six-sigmoid paragraph classifier, 4 epochs, lr
$2{\cdot}10^{-5}$, 512-token windows with stride-128 overflow, max
pooling; Task~2: seven-way segment classifier, same recipe); fp16 GPU
replication of the primary encoder run reproduced digit-for-digit across
duplicated jobs. LLM runs pin provider, model
route, temperature, adapter, and date; the DSPy disk cache makes every
reported number re-derivable without new API calls. Splits, fold seeds,
and threshold-fitting code are released; the official scorers are used
through parity-tested ports. Approximate paid-API ledger: model bake-off
\$3.8; optimizer campaigns ${\approx}$\$4; v3 role campaign
${\approx}$\$13.5; routing/rollout evidence ${\approx}$\$13; verifier,
specialist, and rescue gates ${\approx}$\$6; development probes and
deploy twins ${\approx}$\$6; total ${\approx}$US\$50. Encoder training:
${\approx}$30 Kaggle GPU-hours (screens and multi-seed evidence) plus
CPU runs; the one TAPT measurement added ${\approx}$12 CPU-hours.
Evaluation-phase inference (2026-07-27) was one pass per pinned stage
over the 213-paragraph test input: quote extraction, role decisions on
1{,}912 atoms, five vote rollouts, two co-signal runs, the seed judge
(16 calls) and verifier (45 calls), ${\approx}$3.9k LLM calls in
total, for ${\approx}$US\$10, plus ${\approx}$35 CPU-minutes for the
two encoder deploys; every stage completed error-free on the first
attempt, and the two deploy-twin controls reproduced their development
outputs byte-identically before any test inference ran.
